Problem Framing & Commercial Objective

This document proposes an analytics-driven commercial strategy for Biogen's field organization as it prepares for the subcutaneous (SC) formulation of Leqembi. The framework is designed for a commercial data scientist or customer insights team to execute.

Clinical Context

Leqembi (lecanemab) is initiated via biweekly IV infusion. The proposed SC formulation — if approved — would serve as a maintenance option after IV-phase stabilization. This creates a new commercial question that did not exist at initial IV launch:

The core question: Among all HCPs currently prescribing Leqembi IV, which should the field team prioritize for SC conversion conversations — and why?

Why This Requires Analytics

  • Not all high-IV-prescribers benefit equally from SC
  • SC value is highest where IV creates operational friction
  • Field capacity is finite — precision targeting matters
  • No SC claims data exists at launch — requires inference from analogs
  • Patient-level factors (travel, caregiver burden) vary by geography
Proposal scope: This framework describes what data to acquire, what analysis to conduct, and what commercial decisions the outputs would inform — not results from a completed study.

Proposed Data Strategy: What to Acquire and Why

We recommend building the HCP targeting model from five data domains. Below we specify the recommended data source type, example variables, and the analytical role each domain plays.

Claims / Rx Data

Leqembi IV Utilization

  • HCP-level IV Rx counts (trailing 12 months)
  • New patient starts vs. maintenance patients
  • Patient 6-month treatment retention rate
  • Concomitant anti-amyloid therapy use

Role: Establishes the maintenance-eligible patient pool per HCP — the denominator for any IV→SC conversion opportunity.

Operational / Site Data

Infusion Center Capacity

  • Infusion chair count and hours of operation
  • Average chair utilization rate (%)
  • Wait time for new IV patient scheduling
  • Nursing staff per infusion room ratio

Role: Identifies "infusion friction" — the key driver of SC's operational value proposition. Sites running above ~85% utilization create an urgent conversion incentive.

Geographic / CMS Data

Patient Access & Geography

  • Average drive time from patient zip to infusion site
  • Urban / rural / suburban site classification
  • % Medicare / Medicaid payer mix (proxy for socioeconomic burden)
  • Region-level caregiver availability index

Role: Quantifies patient travel burden. Where travel to infusion is a barrier, SC may prevent drop-off and improve adherence — a distinct patient-centered value driver.

Analog Launch Data

Historical IV→SC Biologic Transitions

  • Ocrevus (ocrelizumab) IV → Ocrevus SC adoption patterns
  • Darzalex (daratumumab) → Darzalex Faspro switch velocity
  • Phesgo (pertuzumab/trastuzumab) conversion rates
  • HCP-level adoption timing from analog launches

Role: Serves as a proxy for SC adoption propensity before real Leqembi SC claims exist. The core method for solving the cold-start targeting problem.

Recommended data vendors: IQVIA Xponent / Symphony Health (claims), MMIT (access/payer), Definitive Healthcare (site capacity), CMS Open Payments and Medicare Part B (utilization benchmarks). Analog data can be extracted from published literature or licensed datasets.

Proposed HCP Segmentation Framework

We propose segmenting the HCP universe into four non-overlapping commercial segments, each defined by distinct data criteria and warranting different field team actions. Segment assignment would be updated quarterly as new claims data matures.

Segment A
High IV Volume Prescribers

HCPs in the top quartile of Leqembi IV Rx volume. Largest pool of eligible maintenance patients for SC conversion conversations.

  • Criteria: Top-25% IV Rx volume (trailing 12M)
  • Data source: Claims / Rx
  • Field action: Introduction + patient identification
~60% of all eligible maintenance patients
Segment B · HIGHEST PRIORITY
Infusion-Constrained Sites

Sites where infusion chair utilization exceeds a defined threshold (e.g., ≥85%). SC adoption here frees capacity for new IV starts, creating a virtuous commercial cycle.

  • Criteria: Chair utilization ≥85% OR wait >4 weeks
  • Data source: Site operational data
  • Field action: Operational ROI pitch + rapid conversion support
Highest conversion value per HCP engagement
Segment C
Early SC Adopter Profiles

HCPs who historically adopted SC formulations early in analogous biologic launches. Identified via analog data — high receptivity to novel delivery innovation.

  • Criteria: Early-adopt rank in ≥2 biologic SC analogs
  • Data source: Analog launch data
  • Field action: Clinical update + KOL activation + samples
Highest expected time-to-first-SC-Rx
Segment D
High Patient Travel Burden

HCPs at sites where the patient geography makes IV maintenance clinically untenable over time. SC offers a continuity of care solution — a distinct patient-centered value story.

  • Criteria: Median patient drive time >30 min to infusion site
  • Data source: CMS zip code + claims linkage
  • Field action: Patient adherence + access support tools
Strongest long-term adherence improvement opportunity
Note on overlap: HCPs may qualify for multiple segments — e.g., a high-volume prescriber who also operates a constrained infusion site. We recommend a priority-ordering rule: Segment B (infusion-constrained) always takes precedence in field team framing, as the ROI of operational unlock is more immediately quantifiable and actionable.

Proposed Analog Analysis Methodology

Because Leqembi SC will launch with zero historical claims data, the targeting model cannot be trained directly on observed SC prescribing behavior. We propose using IV-to-SC biologic analogs to engineer predictive features and estimate relative HCP adoption propensity before real-world data exists.

Why Analogs Work Here

IV-to-SC switches across biologic categories share a common adoption dynamic: early adopters are driven by operational efficiency or patient convenience, while laggards require either peer pressure from KOLs or reimbursement clarity. The specific therapy matters less than the structural adoption pattern.

Published literature on Darzalex Faspro and Ocrevus SC transitions shows that infusion chair utilization is consistently the strongest site-level predictor of early SC adoption, with relative conversion rates 2–3× higher at capacity-constrained sites versus unconstrained sites in the first 6 months post-launch. (See: Mateos et al., 2020; Hauser et al., 2023 — used here as a methodological precedent, not a direct benchmark.)

Proposed analog pool: Darzalex Faspro (oncology), Ocrevus SC (neurology / MS), Phesgo (oncology), Kesimpta (CNS / neurology). We recommend prioritizing neurology analogs (Ocrevus, Kesimpta) for HCP behavior modeling, given specialty alignment.

Proposed Analytical Steps

  1. 1

    Acquire analog Rx panel data

    Obtain HCP-level prescription data for each analog therapy covering 24 months before and after SC formulation launch. Source: IQVIA Xponent or Symphony Health panel.

  2. 2

    Define and measure adoption velocity

    For each HCP in the analog dataset, calculate time-to-first-SC-Rx (in weeks from SC availability), and classify as early / mid / late adopter using quartile cutoffs.

  3. 3

    Identify HCP-level adoption predictors

    Using logistic regression or random forest on the analog cohort, identify which HCP and site characteristics predict early SC adoption. Candidate predictors: IV volume, site capacity, specialty, geography, network centrality.

  4. 4

    Engineer "Analog Propensity Score" for Leqembi HCPs

    Apply the trained feature weights to the current Leqembi IV HCP universe to generate a ranked analog propensity score. This serves as a prior probability for SC adoption at launch.

  5. 5

    Validate and update as real data accumulates

    As Leqembi SC claims emerge post-launch, re-calibrate the model weights using actual observed behavior. Track model lift vs. a non-targeted control group.

Proposed HCP Scoring Model

We propose a composite weighted scoring model — not a black-box ML system — specifically because field teams need to understand and explain every score to HCPs. Each component is independently interpretable and maps directly to a field messaging strategy.

Proposed Score Components & Suggested Weights

Infusion Site Constraint Level35%
Current Leqembi IV Maintenance Volume25%
Analog-Derived SC Propensity Score25%
Patient Travel / Geographic Burden15%
Capacity 35% Volume 25% Analog 25% Travel 15%

Weights are initial hypotheses informed by analog data and clinical judgment. They should be calibrated empirically as Leqembi SC claims mature — e.g., via elastic net regression on early-adopter outcomes.

Model Design Rationale

  • Interpretable by design — each driver maps to a field message
  • No black-box components at launch (explainability > raw accuracy)
  • Weighted additive model allows easy sensitivity analysis
  • Components updated on different refresh cycles (claims quarterly, site data semi-annually)
  • Output is a ranked tier list (Tier 1 / 2 / 3), not a raw number, to limit over-interpretation
Proposed validation approach: Back-test the scoring model against early adopters from analog SC launches (e.g., Ocrevus SC) to assess whether the same feature weights would have correctly ranked early vs. late adopters. Report Spearman rank correlation and top-decile lift as performance benchmarks — not AUC from a trained ML model.
Important framing: This is a prioritization score, not a prediction model. It ranks relative opportunity — it does not forecast Rx probability. This distinction should be communicated clearly to field leadership.

Proposed Success Metrics

We recommend tracking the following KPIs to evaluate whether the targeting strategy is working post-launch. These are proposed measurement frameworks, not predicted outcomes.

Time to First SC Rx
Weeks
Measure median weeks from first field engagement to first SC Rx. Compare Tier 1 vs. Tier 3 HCPs to validate that higher-ranked accounts are converting faster — i.e., whether the score predicts adoption speed.
IV → SC Conversion Rate
%
Track what fraction of active IV maintenance patients at targeted sites transition to SC at 3, 6, and 12 months. Disaggregate by segment to assess which driver (capacity, travel, analog propensity) predicts conversion best.
Capacity Unlock (Infusion Hours)
Hours/wk
At infusion-constrained sites (Segment B), measure change in average weekly infusion chair hours consumed per site as SC transitions accumulate. Quantify the operational ROI directly for KOL and system-level discussions.
Patient Treatment Continuity
Rate
Compare 6-month treatment persistence for patients who switched to SC vs. those who remained on IV. A statistically significant improvement in persistence among SC patients would validate the travel-burden targeting hypothesis.
Field Engagement Efficiency
Ratio
Calls-to-conversion ratio for Tier 1 vs. Tier 3 HCPs. If the model is working, Tier 1 accounts should require fewer rep touchpoints per SC conversion. This is a standard field force productivity metric.
Score Rank Correlation with Adoption
ρ
Spearman rank correlation between the proposed priority score and observed adoption quartile. This is the primary model validity test — computed at 6 and 12 months post-launch to assess whether the ranking correctly orders HCPs.
