About

From the bench to the model

I started in biology. During my B.Tech. in biotechnology at NIT Calicut, and one summer at the ICMR Regional Medical Research Centre in Port Blair, I did bench work: culturing bacteria, running PCR and gels, ELISA, microscopy. Over time I got more interested in what computation could tell us about those systems, and later completed a diploma in data science at IIT Madras.

After graduating I spent two and a half years in pharmaceutical forecasting, building epidemiology-based forecasts for oncology and hematology brands. It was steady work, but it wasn't what I wanted to be doing. I wanted to write code and work on biology, so I went back to school for computer science.

Now I'm doing an M.S. in Computer Science at UMass Amherst, and since July 2026 I've been a Data Science Co-op at Boehringer Ingelheim. On the machine learning side I've worked with protein and genome language models (ESM-2, EvoDiff, Evo 2), embedding models, retrieval-augmented generation, and Apache Spark for data too large for one machine.

I try to build things that hold up: pipelines someone else can rerun, and results with their limits written next to them. My antimicrobial resistance paper is a good example. The genomic foundation model I tested did not beat curated biological features, so that is what the paper reports.

What I work on

  • Antimicrobial resistance Predicting resistance from bacterial genomes, and testing whether foundation models add anything over curated biology.
  • Proteins Protein language models like ESM-2 and generative models like EvoDiff, for representation and design.
  • Language models for science Embedding models and retrieval-augmented generation, and tools that pull sourced, checkable data out of text.

Research

Featured research

Manuscript2026 · Rushath Rajeev · Independent research

Curated resistance gene families outperform Evo 2 embeddings on unseen variants in Escherichia coli

Can a DNA foundation model predict antibiotic resistance better than a curated catalogue of resistance genes? I built a reproducible pipeline to predict minimum inhibitory concentrations (MICs) for ceftriaxone, ciprofloxacin, and gentamicin from E. coli genomes, and compared AMRFinderPlus features with frozen Evo 2 embeddings.

  • Curated 29,619 susceptibility measurements from 11,520 isolates, with traceable sources, exclusion records, and frozen evaluation splits.
  • Fitted censoring-aware ridge regression and XGBoost models with nested cross-validation grouped by lineage. External predictions were locked before the reference MICs were attached.
  • Evo 2 embeddings alone did worse than catalogue features for all three drugs in the locked external test. With four CTX-M alleles held out of training, curated gene-family features beat Evo 2 by 4.6 percentage points (ridge) and 11.1 points (XGBoost).
